## Final lesson
# Check an AI draft before you send it

An AI tool can write a clean, confident draft that is still wrong. Once it goes out under your name, every mistake in it is yours.

## What to do

1. **Read it once for the point.** Before you check any details, decide whether the draft says what you need it to say. Write your main point in one sentence of your own, then see whether the draft makes that point in its first few lines. If it does not, fix the order first, because polishing text in the wrong order wastes time.
2. **Check every fact against where it came from.** Names, dates, numbers, prices and quotes are the details most worth checking, because a wrong one can look just as convincing as a right one. Go back to the original for each: the email thread, meeting notes, contract or report you gave the tool. If a fact is not in any of those, delete it or check it with the person who would know. That is usually the sender of the original email, the project lead for deadlines or the finance contact for figures.
3. **Cut anything you did not ask for.** AI tools often add extras: a promise, an apology, an offer of help. Look for any sentence that commits you or your team to something, such as a date, a discount or a next step. Keep it only if you have the authority to make that promise and you mean to keep it.
4. **Read it out loud as the person receiving it.** Tone problems are easy to miss on screen and hard to miss when you hear them. Listen for phrases you would never say, like stiff openings or too much praise. Replace them with the words you would use if you were talking to that person face to face.
5. **Check who it goes to and what is attached.** Last, look at the To and CC lines. A draft written as a reply to an old thread may still include people who should not see this message. Open each attachment to confirm it is the version you mean to send, not just a file with the right name.

## A quick example

Jordan is a project coordinator who needs to tell a client about a late shipment. Jordan asks an AI tool to draft the update and pastes in the meeting notes. The draft looks polished. Jordan writes the point in one line: "The shipment moves to March 14, and nothing else changes." The draft buries the new date in its third paragraph, so Jordan moves it to the top. Next, Jordan checks the date against the meeting notes and finds the draft says March 12. Jordan corrects it. The draft also offers a 10 percent credit for the delay, which nobody discussed, so Jordan deletes that line. Reading aloud, Jordan hears "We deeply regret any inconvenience this may have caused" and changes it to "Sorry for the delay." Finally, Jordan sees that the draft still copies a contractor who left the project last month, removes that address and sends the update.

## The mistake people make

The most common mistake is checking only the writing. A draft can be smooth, polite and free of typos and still carry the wrong date or a promise nobody agreed to. Clean writing makes these errors easier to miss, because the text feels finished. Check the point, the facts and the commitments before you spend time on style.

   B. The finance contact, since dates affect the budget.
   C. The client, since the email is going to them.
   D. The person leading the project.

   B. Keep it, since the company really does offer this discount.
   C. Soften it to "We may be able to offer you a discount."
   D. Keep it and copy the sales manager so they know.

4. Reading a thank-you note out loud, you hear: "Your truly outstanding, exceptional and tireless work has been nothing short of remarkable." What should you do?
   A. Keep it, since a thank-you note cannot have too much warmth.
   B. Delete the sentence so the note is shorter.
   C. Rewrite it the way you would thank that person if they were standing in front of you.
   D. Ask the AI tool to make the sentence sound warmer.

5. Priya's AI draft is a reply on an email thread that started three months ago. Her facts, promises and tone are all correct. What should she do with the To and CC lines?
   A. Go through each name and remove anyone who should not see this new message.

   C. Add her manager on CC so there is a record.
   D. Move everyone from CC to To so nobody misses the message.
