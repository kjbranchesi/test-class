# Check an AI draft before you send it

An AI tool can write a clean, confident draft that is still wrong.

## What to do

1. **Read it once for the point.**
2. **Check every fact against where it came from.**
3. **Cut anything you did not ask for.**
4. **Read it out loud as the person receiving it.**
5. **Check who it goes to and what is attached.**

## A quick example

Jordan, a project coordinator, catches three problems in an AI draft: a wrong date, a credit nobody approved and an old contact on the CC line.

## The mistake people make

The most common mistake is checking only the writing.

## Quiz

1. Ben's AI draft asks his team to move their files to a new shared folder by Friday. He writes his point in one line and finds it only in the draft's last paragraph. The draft also has a wrong folder name and a stiff opening. What should he fix first?
   A. The wrong folder name, because facts come before style.
   B. Where the request sits, by bringing it up near the start.
   C. The stiff opening, because the team reads it first.
   D. All three together, in one pass through the draft.

2. An AI draft of a status email says the launch deadline is May 2. Neither the meeting notes nor the email thread you gave the tool mentions a deadline. Who should you check the date with before leaving it in?
   A. The AI tool, by asking where it found the date.
   B. The finance contact, since dates affect the budget.
   C. The client, since the email is going to them.
   D. The person leading the project.

3. Without being asked, your AI draft to a vendor says: "We can offer you 5 percent off your next order." Your company does give discounts like this, but only the sales manager can approve them, and that is not you. What should you do?
   A. Take the line out.
   B. Keep it, since the company really does offer this discount.
   C. Soften it to "We may be able to offer you a discount."
   D. Keep it and copy the sales manager so they know.

4. Reading a thank-you note out loud, you hear: "Your truly outstanding, exceptional and tireless work has been nothing short of remarkable." What should you do?
   A. Keep it, since a thank-you note cannot have too much warmth.
   B. Delete the sentence so the note is shorter.
   C. Rewrite it the way you would thank that person if they were standing in front of you.
   D. Ask the AI tool to make the sentence sound warmer.

5. Priya's AI draft is a reply on an email thread that started three months ago. Her facts, promises and tone all check out. What should she do with the To and CC lines?
   A. Go through each name and remove anyone who should not see this new message.
   B. Leave them as they are, since the thread already has the right people.
   C. Add her manager on CC so there is a record.
   D. Move everyone from CC to To so nobody misses the message.
