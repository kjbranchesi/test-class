# Check an AI Draft Before You Send It

An AI tool can write a clean draft in seconds, but it can also state wrong facts in a confident voice. Once you send the draft, you are responsible for every error in it.

## What to do

1. **Read it as if you wrote it.** Read the whole draft from start to finish before you send it. Ask yourself: would I say this, in these words, to this person? If a sentence does not sound like you or promises too much, rewrite it.
2. **Check every fact against its source.** A fact is any claim that could be true or false, such as a status, a policy or a promise. Names, dates, numbers, prices and quotes are all facts. The source is the place the fact really comes from, such as the original email, the contract, the report or the calendar. Open that source and compare. If you cannot find a source for a fact, take it out or confirm it with someone who knows, usually the person who sent the original email or owns the contract.
3. **Look for things the AI added.** AI tools sometimes fill gaps with details that sound right but were never in your request, like a deadline, a discount or a promise. Compare the draft to what you asked for. Delete anything you did not give it and cannot confirm.
4. **Remove anything private.** Make sure the draft does not include details the recipient should not see. These include another customer's name, internal numbers or notes you pasted in as background. AI tools often reuse whatever you gave them.
5. **Read it once more as the recipient.** Picture the person opening the message. Is the main point in the first two lines? Is any request clear? Cut anything they do not need.

## A quick example

Priya asked an AI tool to reply to a client about a late order. The draft had the wrong ship date, a discount she never offered and a line about another client's order. The draft is polite and clear. She reads it through and changes "We deeply regret this unacceptable failure" to "Sorry for the delay." Next she checks the facts. The draft says the order ships on March 14, but the shipping tracker says March 18. She fixes the date. She notices the draft offers "a 10% discount for the trouble." She never mentioned a discount, so she deletes it. She also finds a line about another client's order that came from notes she pasted in. She removes it. Last, she moves the new ship date to the first sentence, because that is what the client most wants to know.

## The mistake people make

The most common mistake is checking only the parts that look wrong. AI drafts read smoothly, so errors do not stand out. A wrong date looks just like a right one. People skim, see clean sentences and send. The fix is to check every fact against its source, even the ones that look fine, because a smooth sentence is not proof that it is true.
