## Final lesson

# Make a Batch of Cold Brew Coffee at Home

One batch of cold brew takes about ten minutes of hands-on work and gives you coffee for most of the work week. It saves money, and you do not need to stop to buy coffee on your way to work.

## What to do

1. **Grind the coffee coarse.** Use beans ground about as coarse as raw sugar or breadcrumbs. Fine grounds make the coffee bitter and muddy and clog the strainer. If you buy ground coffee, ask the coffee shop or store for a coarse grind.
2. **Mix one part coffee with four parts water, by volume.** For a large jar, that is 1 cup of coarse grounds to 4 cups of cold or room-temperature water. Measure with cups, not a scale, because weighing gives a much stronger batch. Stir until all the grounds are wet. Do not use hot water, because heat pulls out the bitter flavors cold brew is meant to avoid.
3. **Steep it for 12 to 24 hours, depending on where it sits.** Cover the jar. On the counter, steep it for 12 to 18 hours. In the fridge, steep it for about 18 to 24 hours. Less time than that gives weak coffee. After 24 hours, it starts to taste bitter and woody.
4. **Strain it twice.** Pour it through a fine mesh strainer to catch the grounds, then through a paper coffee filter to catch the fine silt. What you have now is a concentrate, not a drink.
5. **Dilute and store.** Before drinking, mix the concentrate with an equal amount of water or milk and pour it over ice. Keep the undiluted concentrate in a sealed jar in the fridge and use it within about a week.

## A quick example

On Sunday at 7 p.m., Priya grinds 1 cup of beans coarse and stirs them into 4 cups of tap water in a large jar. She covers it and leaves it on the counter. On Monday at 9 a.m., 14 hours later, she strains it through a mesh strainer and then a paper filter. She gets about 3 cups of concentrate and puts it in the fridge. Each morning she pours about half a cup of concentrate into a glass, adds the same amount of milk, then adds ice. The jar lasts her through Friday.

## The mistake people make

The most common mistake is drinking the concentrate straight. It tastes strong and harsh, and people decide cold brew is not for them, or they add extra water to the jar on the next batch and end up with weak, flat coffee. Keep the one-to-four recipe and dilute in the glass instead, half concentrate and half water or milk. If it is still too strong, add more water to the glass, not to the jar.
